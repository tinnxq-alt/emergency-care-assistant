# Clinical verification audit log

## 2026-08-18 · v0.15

Purpose: re-check and refine existing high-risk clinical content before adding more topics. This audit does not certify the site as a medical device or replace local protocols, product labels, specialist consultation, or full guidelines.

### Re-verified / refined in this audit

- Hypoglycemia
  - Corrected severity wording to ADA 2026 Level 1 / 2 / 3 framework.
  - Kept the 15 g / 15 min approach for most alert patients who can safely swallow.
  - Separated severe/assistance-required hypoglycemia from glucose-number-only classification.

- Sepsis / septic shock
  - Re-checked against 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign.
  - Refined antibiotic timing: shock or probable/definite sepsis → immediate, ideally within 1 hour; possible sepsis without shock → time-limited rapid investigation and, if concern persists, within 3 hours.
  - Added peripheral vasopressor start when appropriate rather than delaying for central access.
  - Added dynamic fluid responsiveness, capillary refill, serial lactate and individualized post-bolus resuscitation.
  - Retained initial 30 mL/kg as a conditional recommendation, not a universal fixed prescription.

- DKA / HHS
  - Re-checked against the 2024 ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS consensus report.
  - Added initial fluid rates for adults without cardiac/renal compromise and smaller 250 mL bolus strategy for older adults / heart failure / end-stage kidney disease.
  - Added HHS glucose, sodium and osmolality correction-rate limits.
  - Added bicarbonate and phosphate boundaries.

- Acute ischemic stroke
  - Re-checked against the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline and emergency-physician take-home messages.
  - Added alteplase/tenecteplase equivalence within the 4.5-hour IVT window for eligible patients.
  - Added the distinction between disabling and non-disabling stroke.
  - Added selected extended-window IVT using advanced imaging.
  - Added the safety point that intensive SBP lowering to <140 mmHg after reperfusion is not recommended.

- Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage
  - Re-checked major 2022 AHA/ASA safety boundaries.
  - Reinforced smooth BP control, anticoagulant reversal, avoidance of routine prophylactic antiseizure therapy, steroids and continuous prophylactic hyperosmolar therapy.
  - Added that severity scores should not be the sole basis for limiting life-sustaining treatment.

- Adult community-acquired pneumonia
  - Re-checked NICE NG250, including January 2026 update.
  - Added high-severity inpatient corticosteroid duration (4–7 days or until discharge, whichever comes first) and hydrocortisone-first consideration.
  - Added the fluoroquinolone + corticosteroid safety warning.

- Bacterial meningitis / meningococcal disease
  - Re-checked NICE NG240.
  - Reinforced hospital antibiotic target within 1 hour.
  - Added dexamethasone timing when delayed (<12 h vs >12 h).
  - Added pathogen-specific course examples that are explicitly present in NICE (pneumococcus 10 d, meningococcus 5 d, Listeria 21 d), with infection-specialist/local-guidance caveats.

- Adrenal crisis
  - Re-checked NICE NG243.
  - Confirmed immediate hydrocortisone 100 mg IV/IM, 0.9% sodium chloride 1 L over ~30 min, then 200 mg/24 h or 50 mg q6h until stable.
  - Added NICE oral stress-dose step-down after stabilization.

- Acute pancreatitis
  - Confirmed ACG 2024 remains the current ACG acute pancreatitis guideline as of this audit.
  - Retained moderate/dynamic fluid strategy, early feeding, no prophylactic antibiotics, selective ERCP.
  - Added same-admission / pre-discharge cholecystectomy for mild biliary pancreatitis when fit for surgery.

### Explicitly kept partially verified / source-bound

- COPD acute exacerbation: GOLD 2026 source remains current, but exact steroid/antibiotic/device parameters are not newly hard-coded unless directly re-checked from the primary report.
- Severe symptomatic hyponatremia, acute hypocalcemia and acute hypercalcemia: existing Society for Endocrinology guidance remains in use; these are queued for a dedicated electrolyte audit because source age and local 3% saline/calcium preparations matter.
- Toxicology cards: AHA 2025 remains the primary source; exact sodium bicarbonate, high-dose insulin, lipid emulsion and antidote infusion protocols stay partially locked where local concentration/monitoring requirements are essential.
- Drug compatibility, pump concentration/rate and formulation-specific instructions remain local/label dependent even when the underlying emergency indication is guideline verified.

### Safety rule

Availability in the ward pharmacy or crash cart never changes guideline recommendation priority or verification status.
